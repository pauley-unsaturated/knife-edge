## 1. Taxonomy of core TD mechanics

### 1a. Spatial models

| Model | Definition | Canonical examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed path** | Enemies follow a designer-drawn road; towers sit beside it (often in fixed slots). | Kingdom Rush (fixed build slots), Bloons TD, Vector TD, Cursed Treasure, Fieldrunners' path maps |
| **Mazing / open-field** | No pre-set path; creeps take the shortest route through whatever the player builds. Towers ARE the walls. | Desktop Tower Defense, Fieldrunners' open-field maps, Infinitode 2, Dungeon Warfare (barricades), Emberward (tetromino walls) |
| **Open field + walls** | Cheap non-attacking blockers shape the path; towers do damage. | Dungeon Warfare 1/2 ("free placement of traps with barricades enabling mazing"), Emberward, Element TD (tower-mazing) |
| **Lane defense** | Enemies advance in discrete rows; defenders occupy grid cells in each lane. | Plants vs Zombies. George Fan "found enemies ignoring the defensive structures unintuitive, which led to the use of lanes" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plants_vs._Zombies_(video_game))) |
| **Tower + hero** | Player-controlled unit(s) supplement static defenses. | Kingdom Rush (hero + reinforcements + spells), Dungeon Defenders (hero build vs. tower build tension), Lock's Quest (DS, 2008), Protect Me Knight |
| **Builder / city-defense** | Economy buildings and defenses share space/budget; day-build/night-defend loop. | Thronefall, Diplomacy is Not an Option, Kingdom Two Crowns, The Last Spell, Dome Keeper |
| **Rampart (1990)** | Territory-enclosure puzzle + artillery duel. | The 8/16-bit ancestor (see below) |

**Desktop Tower Defense (Paul Preece, March 2007)** is the reference mazing game. Preece was motivated by "a lack of tower defense games that allowed proper 'mazing'"; "the path of the creeps is not set; instead, the towers built determine the path." The game also codified **juggling**: "alternating between barricading an exit on one side and then the other side to cause the enemies to path back and forth" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desktop_Tower_Defense)). It ran 50 levels, with creep classes **Immune** (can't be slowed), **Spawn** (splits on death), **Flying** (ignores the maze), **Dark** (flat armor), and boss variants of each; towers included Swarm (anti-air only), Frost (slow), Bash (AoE + stun) ([Kongregate wiki](https://kongregate.fandom.com/wiki/Desktop_Tower_Defense)). A design review notes DTD "awards extra scores for sending the next wave earlier, making the game harder" ([Game Design Reviews](http://gamedesignreviews.com/reviews/desktop-tower-defense-perfect-job/)).

**Fieldrunners (Subatomic, Oct 2008)** was iOS's first hit TD: "an innovative merger of tactical paths and open field based gameplay," 11 weapon types, IGF Mobile Best Game 2009 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fieldrunners/id292421271), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_Studios)).

**Rampart, the precise phase loop** (Atari Games arcade, 1990; ports: NES by Bitmasters/Jaleco 1992, SNES by Bitmasters/EA Aug 1992, Game Boy, Game Boy Color 1999):

1. **Cannon placement**: a short timer to place additional cannons *inside enclosed territory* (if you left room). ([Arcade Museum](https://www.arcade-museum.com/Videogame/rampart))
2. **Battle**: aim a crosshair, fire at ships (and, in multiplayer, at rivals' walls). Enemy cannonballs blow holes in your walls. "Double-masted ships will release little tanks called 'grunts' onto the field if they hit the shoreline." Lasts "about ten seconds" until the cabinet announces **CEASE FIRE**. ([Set Side B](https://setsideb.com/arcade-mermaid-rampart-part-3-the-phases/))
3. **Build and Repair**: a stream of *random* wall pieces ("superficially similar to Tetris shapes" but ranging from 1x1 squares to plus- and U-shapes) placed under a timer. Enclosing a castle "captures" its territory. **Grunts move during Building and can block piece placement.** Lose condition: fail to fully enclose at least one castle before the timer expires. ([StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Rampart/Gameplay), [Hardcore Gaming 101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/rampart/))
4. Loop back to 1; ships and fleet size escalate.

Why it matters for a throwback: the loop already contains *repair-under-pressure*, *random piece draw*, *territory = build capacity*, and *enemies that persist across phases*. Wikipedia and several histories name it "the first recognized tower defense game" ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Rampart), [WellGames history](https://wellgames.com/news/tower-defense-games-evolution-126087/)).

### 1b. Mob generation

- **Timed waves + "call early" bonus.** Kingdom Rush: calling early grants "free Gold equal to the number of seconds left before the wave would have come in naturally, and added recharge to spells equal to the number of seconds left"; the *Golden Time* meta-upgrade raises that bonus 80% ([Kingdom Rush wiki](https://kingdomrushtd.fandom.com/wiki/Upgrades)). DTD used the same lever for score.
- **Enemy sending / competitive.** Legion TD 2 splits currency: gold for fighters, **mythium** for sending mercenaries at opponents or upgrading your king; "your income increases immediately and permanently whenever you spend mythium," and "every wave, your income increases by 5 per 20 mythium." Workers cost 50 gold and yield 1 mythium / 10 s. 21 waves; "most games will end before wave 21" ([LTD2 wiki: Income](https://legiontd2.fandom.com/wiki/Income), [Worker](https://legiontd2.fandom.com/wiki/Worker)). Element TD (WC3) pioneered element-vs-armor sends; Element TD 2 has an element pick "at the start of the game and each 5 waves" up to wave 50 (11 picks), choosing an element *or* extra income/essence ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2360427994)).
- **Spawner buildings / persistent enemies.** Loop Hero's monster-spawning terrain cards; Kingdom Two Crowns' Greed portals; Rampart's grunts.
- **Endless / scaling.** Infinitode 2: "every game is endless"; an Endless-mode research layer allows "over-leveling towers for exponential growth necessary to survive infinitely scaling enemies" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/937310/), [Shapes wiki](https://shapes.inc/fandom/infinitode-2-infinite-tower-defense/research-tree)). Dome Keeper scales wave strength by cycle, *total resources mined*, and prior-wave performance ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2869939597)).
- **Enemy types with counters** (canonical property sets):
  - Bloons TD 6: Camo (needs detection, appears round 24), Lead (immune to sharp), Ceramic (10-damage shell), Fortified (2x outer layer), Regrow, MOAB-class (DDT = Black+Lead+Camo, 400 HP, spawns 4 camo-regrow ceramics; BAD = 20,000 HP) ([Blooncyclopedia](https://www.bloonswiki.com/Camo_Bloon_(BTD6)), [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1608475039)).
  - Infinitode 2: Regular (1 tile/s), Fast (+25%, weak to AoE), Strong (tanky + resistances), **Armored** (blocks half damage to allies within 1.5 tiles), **Healer** (5% max HP/s to nearby), bosses at each stage's final level ([Infinitode 2 wiki](https://infinitode-2.fandom.com/wiki/Enemies)).
  - DTD: Immune / Spawn / Flying / Dark + bosses (above).

### 1c. Weapon / tower systems

- **Element / combination.** Element TD: six elements (Fire, Earth, Light, Nature, Water, Darkness); 15 dual + 20 triple towers in the WC3 map; Element TD 2 advertises 59 towers. Armor wheel: "each elemental armor takes additional damage from the element preceding it and retains near-invulnerability from the element following it" ([w3reforged](https://maps.w3reforged.com/maps/categories/tower-defense-td/element-td-survivor), [Maul Tactics](https://maultactics.gg/articles/wc3-custom-maps-standalone-games)). GemCraft: towers don't attack, *gems* do; pure gems boost special, dual gems boost damage, 3+ components dilute specials; "supergemming" merges many low-grade gems to raise damage/fire-rate without raising grade ([GemCraft wiki: Gems](https://gemcraft.fandom.com/wiki/Gems)).
- **Upgrade trees vs. flat upgrades.** Kingdom Rush: four families, branching tier-4 specialisations; Cursed Treasure 2: three trees (Orc/Undead/Demon), 8 upgrades per tree yielding 27 tower variants ([GameClub](https://gameclub.io/stories/top-10-skills-in-cursed-treasure-2)). Vector TD / DTD: flat linear levels (Vector TD: 10 levels per tower).
- **Targeting priority as a lever.** BTD6's First/Last/Close/Strong: "Strong" maximizes single-target snipers but leaves stragglers; "Last" on straightaways maximizes pierce; "First" for cleanup ([Bloons wiki](https://bloons.fandom.com/wiki/Targeting_Priority)).
- **Sell / refund.** Vector TD 75%; BTD6 70% (75% with Better Sell Deals), older Bloons 80%; Kingdom Rush has an upgrade pushing refund to 90% of cumulative cost (baseline not found in search) ([Bloons wiki: Selling](https://bloons.fandom.com/wiki/Selling), [KR Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2812921847)).
- **Interest / razor-thin economies.**
  - **Vector TD (David Scott, Candystand, June 2007)** is the canonical case. Kills pay **$4 + current wave number**; sells refund **75%**; 11 towers x 10 upgrade levels; 50 waves. Interest "earns you dividends on any money you have remaining when a new wave begins." Bonus points (from killing the power-cell vectoid in each Hard Grey wave) buy +damage, +range, **+interest**, or +5 lives. Guides: "by wave 44 you should be getting 27% interest... if you spend every bonus point... in extra interest, you end up with 33%"; "always spend interest whenever you get a bonus, otherwise there is absolutely no way to get a high score" ([Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/vector_td), [Perception Is Truth](https://perceptionistruth.com/2012/02/beating-vector-td-easy-and-normal/), [JayIsGames](https://jayisgames.com/review/vector-td.php), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_TD)). The *base* interest rate was not found in search excerpts.
  - **Element TD (WC3)**: "every 15 seconds you get extra cash based on your unspent gold... starts at 2.0%"; lumber (1 per 5 rounds) buys interest ([EleTD forums](https://forums.eletd.com/topic/945-basics-of-element-td/)).
  - Desktop TD: no interest mechanic surfaced in search; its economy tension is instead the early-call score bonus and placement.
  - Design principle: interest forces under-building early and rewards precise timing; Legion TD 2's permanent-income-on-spend is the competitive analogue.

### 1d. Progression

- **In-run:** gold/interest (Vector TD, Element TD), tech picks (Element TD every 5 waves), card draws (Rogue Tower), tile draws (Isle of Arrows), talents/relics (Emberward), day-phase buildings (Thronefall, Last Spell).
- **Meta:** Infinitode 2's 400+ node research tree ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/937310/)); Kingdom Rush star trees; Rogue Tower XP unlocks; Dungeon Warfare 2 skill points + per-map tier; Thronefall XP to weapons/perks/mutators; Loop Hero camp; Last Spell Oraculum.

## 3. NES/SNES-era constraints for authenticity

**NES (Ricoh 2A03/2C02 PPU)**: resolution **256x240** (top/bottom 8 rows usually overscanned, effective ~256x224); **8x8 tiles**; nametable 32x30 tiles; **attribute table assigns one 4-color palette per 16x16-pixel block**, which is why NES games think in **16x16 metatiles** ("for the NES, metatile grids should be set to 16x16px to adhere to attribute table restrictions"; many games layer to 32x32) ([Mega Cat: Metatiles](https://megacatstudios.com/blogs/retro-development/metatiles)). Palettes: 64-color master; 4 background + 4 sprite subpalettes, each **3 colors + shared backdrop/transparent**, so 25 simultaneous colors (13 BG incl. backdrop + 12 sprite). Sprites: **64 total, 8 per scanline** (more = flicker or dropout), 8x8 or 8x16 mode ([Set Side B](https://setsideb.com/behind-the-code-about-the-nes-sprite-capabilities/), [nesdev forum](https://forums.nesdev.org/viewtopic.php?t=16323), [kindatechnical PPU guide](https://kindatechnical.com/retro-nes-game-development/understanding-nes-ppu-picture-processing-unit.html)). **Audio: 5 channels**: 2 pulse, 1 triangle, 1 noise, 1 DPCM sample ([NESdev: APU](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/APU)).

**Design consequence for a TD:** dozens of creeps + projectiles quickly exceed 8 sprites per scanline; era-authentic solutions are background-tile "sprites" for towers and walls, sprite multiplexing/flicker, small waves, and 16x16 build grids.

**SNES**: **256x224** (or 256x239); 8x8 base tiles, BG tiles 8x8 or 16x16; sprites 8x8/16x16/32x32/64x64 (two sizes selectable at once); **128 sprites, 32 per scanline, 34 8x8 sprite tiles per line**; **8 sprite palettes of 15 colors + transparent** (4bpp), 8 BG palettes of 15 in Mode 1; 256 CGRAM entries from a 32,768-color BGR555 master ([SNESdev: Sprites](https://snes.nesdev.org/wiki/Sprites), [SNESdev: Palettes](https://snes.nesdev.org/wiki/Palettes), [Mega Cat SNES guide](https://megacatstudios.com/blogs/retro-development/snes-sprite-engine-design-guidelines)). **Audio: SPC700, 8 sample channels**, 32 kHz, hardware echo/ADSR ([S-SMP](https://snes.nesdev.org/wiki/S-SMP)). **Game Boy**: 4 channels (2 pulse, wave, noise) ([Pan Docs](https://gbdev.io/pandocs/Audio.html)); GBC assigns palettes per 8x8 tile, not 16x16 ([nesdev forum](https://forums.nesdev.org/viewtopic.php?t=17319)).

## 4. "Fun core" hypotheses across the genre

1. **Mazing as authorship**: the map is a canvas; the plan *is* the maze. DTD, Emberward ("same satisfaction as Tetris"), Dungeon Warfare barricades, Rogue Tower's steerable path. Failure mode: solved-maze staleness; Emberward reviewers call it "very easy once you learn it."
2. **Economy tension / interest greed**: deliberately under-building to compound interest, then spending at the last safe moment. Vector TD (27-33% interest by late game), Element TD (2% per 15 s), Legion TD 2 (income-vs-power mythium), Kingdom Rush's early-call gold, Dome Keeper's "sneak a few more blocks" mining clock.
3. **Combinatorial discovery**: elements/gems/cards that multiply into builds. Element TD (6 elements to 59 towers), GemCraft supergemming, Rogue Tower ("freeze builds, splash builds, single-target DPS builds all play differently"), Emberward relics.
4. **The plan holds (spectacle payoff)**: "players immediately see the impact of good decisions"; "the satisfaction of watching a well-built defence be tested by a huge amount of enemies is a classic feel-good factor" ([Stardock dev journal](https://www.stardock.com/games/article/495008/siege-of-centauri-dev-journal-what-makes-a-good-tower-defense-game), [Design the Game](https://www.designthegame.com/learning/tutorial/tower-defense-design-guide)). Bad North's auto-resolving squads and Loop Hero's auto-battler are this hypothesis pushed to the limit. Caveat from the same sources: watching "can't make up too much of the game."
5. **Counter-puzzle reading**: waves are riddles with typed answers (camo/lead/ceramic; immune/spawn/flying/dark; armored/healer supports). BTD6, DTD, Infinitode 2, Element TD's armor wheel.
6. **Draw luck as replayability**: board-game randomness that keeps hands fresh without meta bloat. Isle of Arrows (guaranteed tile ratios soften variance), Rampart's random wall pieces, Emberward tetrominoes, Rogue Tower card offers.
7. **Repair under pressure / the persistent siege**: damage carries across phases and must be triaged. Rampart's build-and-repair with grunts blocking placement, Thronefall's destroyed houses that skip income, Kingdom's coin theft, Dome Keeper's dome HP.
8. **Push-your-luck with permadeath stakes**: "the nagging urge to push things a little further" (Bad North, likened to FTL); Loop Hero's retreat-or-continue; Dome Keeper's resource-scaled waves.

## Takeaways for this project

The most-shipped iOS+desktop roguelike TDs (Isle of Arrows, Dungeon Warfare 2, Infinitode 2, Thronefall, Bad North, Loop Hero) all keep runs to 20-45 minutes, randomize the *offer* (tiles/cards/relics) rather than the rules, and pair a tight in-run economy with a modest unlock layer. Rampart's four-phase loop plus a Vector TD-style interest economy is a natural, historically grounded chassis that also respects NES sprite budgets (walls and towers as background tiles; only creeps and shots as sprites).
