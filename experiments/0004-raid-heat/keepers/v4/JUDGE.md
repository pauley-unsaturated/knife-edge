# v4 verdict: ready for human trial; fun remains unproven

KEEP the six evidenced avenues and paid reroll. The game now has affordable choices with materially different consequences, offer-led investment, distinct successful engines, and a late wall that ordinary unboosted defenses generally cannot beat. This clears a **mechanical readiness** gate, not a subjective enjoyment or final-balance gate.

Difficulty comparisons within each cohort are paired. Forty games give wide confidence intervals; exact intervals accompany the retained reports.

Easy is an onramp, not a knife-edge mode. Medium is the initial groove-finding trial; Raid is more forgiving than Compound/Pact for the learner. Hard is the stronger-planner challenge: Engineer median cleared wave is 14 / 13 / 14. A deliberate 80-tick oracle-assisted controller wins Hard 12 / 4 / 11, showing that slower execution can still work. Neither model represents a measured human IQ, experience percentile, or motor dexterity. Even the non-oracle learner has exact geometric route scoring; the original apprentice's affordable-only starvation makes it an adversarial diagnostic, not a credible novice calibration.

First-wave safety survives; Medium wave 2 leaks in 15/40 Engineer seeds and 20/40 learner seeds per branch. Wave 3 is not uniformly tough. Do not promise the requested early difficulty rhythm on every board.

## Decisions, groove, and the late wall

The 14 retained checkpoints verify independently with no rejected inputs. Poison/Ember, Conductive/Arc, and corpse explosion each have a winning adaptive run that loses when its specific effect is removed while costs remain. Real offered alternatives establish Compound versus immediate slow, Last Stand versus poison, and early sacrifice versus a thermal boost as consequential. The reroll pair starts at wave 8 with 4 core and 53 gold: paying 25 wins at 4 core; keeping visible Conductive loses during wave 14. That demonstrates one rescue, not that rerolling is always correct.

In fresh Easy/Medium learner runs, 416 of 421 typed-or-Cold offers picked without an existing matching investment produce a matching tower within two waves. This is evidence that drops can be acted on and lead allocation, not evidence that the bot experiences a groove or understands every rare status.

Across the original held-out Medium neutral-drop controls, forced Arc wins 0/120 and forced Ember 1/120. Disabling a particular winning effect can be survived by pivoting in some seeds; this is desirable flexibility. Five selected engine winners all die with every command frozen after wave 12. This rejects routine unattended early coasting in those witnesses, not all possible early god builds.

Fixed board 7 with 24 new loot streams yields prepared-driver wins Raid 22, Compound 16, Pact 8. Pact strongly supports “only sometimes it works”; Raid's favorable board does not. Medium Engineer wins 63–83%, so rare victory is not a global property for this strong controller. The weaker learner's Medium Compound/Pact failures typically clear 15 waves before losing, after real build commitments.

## What human play must falsify

- Start Easy to learn the combinations, then try Medium Compound or Pact; use Hard when Medium feels routine. Try several board seeds, rather than judging only favorable board 7. Fixed-board loot variation is a harness experiment, not a separate selector in the demo.
- After the second and third drops, describe the build you think they suggest. Could you afford to follow them? Did a tempting alternative make you hesitate? Did you pivot deliberately?
- Around waves 10–13, ask whether the build feels powerful because of a legible interaction, or merely because many generic towers accumulated. A win without an understandable “this is my build” moment is a design failure even if the simulation passes.
- Try saving for an upgrade, adding a matching coating tower, and paying for one weak-offer reroll. Note whether the foregone defense is visible and the payoff understandable. Record unwanted forced picks, dead offers, and losses whose cause is unclear.
- After losing, ask whether you want another seed and can name one decision you would change. Bored wins, opaque deaths, or repeated identical purchases fail the fun gate and justify the next explicit iteration.

Remaining unproven claims: independent strategic necessity of coatings or Mutiny, repeated-Compound exponential god scaling, interesting permanent raid exclusions, and real-human skill calibration. Last Stand can activate through accidental leaks; not every recovery is a deliberate sacrifice plan. A late Pact offer with only one survivable card is not a meaningful choice. Keep historical checkpoints, but never substitute their old rules for these v4 witnesses. Do not retune on the completed held-out cohort and continue calling it held-out validation.
